Robot Arm Load & Link-Length Calculator

An offline browser tool answering two sizing questions for a serial robot arm: how much can this geometry lift, and how long can the links be for a target payload? It evaluates bending stress, actuator torque, and tip deflection together and names whichever limit binds first.

Why I built it

Hobby robot arms often fail after printing because three coupled effects get ignored during rough sizing:

  • Coupled chain loads: every joint carries everything outboard of it, so wrist mass penalises every joint underneath.
  • Stall torque vs working torque: servo kg·cm ratings are stall figures at maximum current and heat, not continuous working capacity.
  • Stiffness governs printed links: plastic links sag out of tolerance long before yielding, and each link's end slope compounds down the chain.

Engineering model

The arm is evaluated in its worst-case pose (fully extended horizontally) against three independent checks, with the smallest limit governing:

  • 1. Bending stress: six solid and hollow cross-sections checked against Z × yield × k / FoS, where k accounts for FDM print anisotropy.
  • 2. Actuator torque: available torque equals rated × qty × derate × gear ratio × η, defaulting to a 0.50 derate so stall ratings are never treated as working limits.
  • 3. Tip deflection: each link is solved as a cantilever under tip force, outboard moment, and self-weight, summing local sag plus carried slope across all joints.

Key design decisions

  • Automatic component mass: selecting a motor for joint Ji+1 places its mass at the distal end of link i automatically (J1 sits on the base and does not load the arm).
  • Two solve directions: Mode 1 evaluates payload for fixed links, whilst Mode 2 bisects total reach until worst utilisation hits 1.00.
  • Payload CoG offset: adds grip overhang to every joint's lever arm, making its torque penalty obvious.
  • Zero dependencies: inline SVG diagrams, CSV export, and URL-hash state sharing work completely offline.

Limitations

Idealised Euler-Bernoulli beam theory in one static pose excludes:

  • Joint stress concentrations, fastener pull-out, and servo horn/shaft strength.
  • Torsion during base slewing and buckling of compression linkages.
  • Gearbox backlash, bearing play, PLA creep, and thermal softening near warm motors.
  • End-effector grip force (handled separately by the gripper tools).

Built-in actuator ratings are indicative class values, not datasheet guarantees.

Validation plan

  1. Hang known test masses in the horizontal pose and measure tip droop with a dial indicator.
  2. Back-calculate the real print knockdown factor k from measured stiffness.
  3. Hold a loaded pose for one hour to quantify PLA/PETG creep.
  4. Verify final joint geometry and fastener stress concentrations in FEA.

What the force figures mean

  1. Torque → nut force. Motor torque at the screw is converted to axial force using screw lead and the assumed overall efficiency.
  2. Nut → two jaws. The instantaneous opening-to-nut-travel ratio sets the force available at each of two symmetric tips by virtual work.
  3. Grip length → moment. Force at an offset contact point creates a bending moment at each carriage. In this rigid model, the offset does not itself reduce horizontal force.
  4. Actual grip. Force is transmitted to an object only after contact. Before contact, the displayed figure is available force at that opening, not applied force.

The displayed force is not a holding-force or motor-rating guarantee. It excludes motor torque–speed behaviour, structural deflection, local contact mechanics and measured friction losses.

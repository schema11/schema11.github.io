Arm Tool Explanation

A complete build specification for a two-tab Excel workbook. Tab 1 takes a geometry and returns the maximum payload. Tab 2 takes a payload and returns the maximum permissible length of every link. Every formula below is copy-paste ready.

1 · Modelling approach

A serial arm is a coupled chain: each joint carries the weight and moment of everything distal to it. Both tabs model the worst-case pose - fully extended, straight, horizontal, gravity perpendicular to the links - and test three independent limits at every joint.

① Bending stress

Will the link snap? Compares applied moment against section modulus × allowable stress.

② Actuator torque

Can the motor hold the pose? Compares applied moment against peak torque × gear ratio × efficiency.

③ Tip deflection

Will it droop or wobble? Superposed cantilever deflection plus carried slope from every inboard link.

The true capability is the minimum of the three. For FDM-printed arms, ③ almost always governs long before ① does.

Governing equations

Moment demand at joint i, with dynamic amplification \(K_d\):

$$M_i = K_d\,g\left[P\,S_i + \sum_{j>i}\left(m_j\left(S_i-S_j+\tfrac{L_j}{2}\right) + P_j\left(S_i-S_j+L_j\right)\right)\right]$$

Capacity is the lesser of material and motor:

$$M_{cap,i} = \min\!\left(\underbrace{Z_i\,\frac{\sigma_{y,i}\,k}{\text{FoS}}}_{\text{bending}},\ \underbrace{T_{motor,i}\cdot i_{gear}\cdot \eta}_{\text{actuator}}\right)$$

Rearranged for payload, the limit imposed by joint i:

$$P_{max,i} = \frac{M_{cap,i}/(K_d\,g) - \Sigma_i}{S_i} \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad P_{max} = \min_i P_{max,i}$$

2 · Constraint logic

flowchart TD A[Geometry, material, motors] --> B[Section properties A, I, c, Z] B --> C[Link masses and reach to tip] C --> D[Self-weight moment at each joint] D --> E{Capacity check} E --> F[Bending: Z x allowable stress] E --> G[Torque: T x ratio x efficiency] F --> H[Governing capacity = MIN of the two] G --> H H --> I[Payload limit per joint] I --> J[Load limit = MIN across all joints] J --> K{Tip deflection within budget?} K -- Yes --> L[Usable payload confirmed] K -- No --> M[Stiffen section, shorten link, or reduce payload] M --> B

4 · Cross-section geometry

Six section types are supported. b is the width, h is the height in the bending plane (vertical, in the worst-case pose), D is outside diameter and t is wall thickness.

Stiffness scales with h³ in bending - doubling the height of a link multiplies its bending stiffness by eight, while doubling the width only doubles it. A hollow section removes material from near the neutral axis where it contributes almost nothing, which is why tubes dominate on strength-to-weight.

$$I_{rect}=\frac{bh^3}{12} \qquad I_{tube}=\frac{\pi\left(D^4-d^4\right)}{64} \qquad Z=\frac{I}{c} \qquad \sigma=\frac{M}{Z}$$

Deflection block (columns AB–AF, same rows)

Each link is a cantilever carrying a tip force, an applied end moment from everything outboard, and its own distributed weight. The slope at the end of each link is then carried out to the tip and added - this is what makes a long arm droop far more than any single link calculation suggests.

$$\delta=\frac{FL^3}{3EI}+\frac{M_eL^2}{2EI}+\frac{wL^4}{8EI} \qquad \theta=\frac{FL^2}{2EI}+\frac{M_eL}{EI}+\frac{wL^3}{6EI}$$

$$\delta_{tip}=\sum_i \delta_i + \sum_i \theta_i\,S_{i+1}$$

First-pass sanity check

For a single link, ignoring self-weight:

$$L_{max}\approx\frac{1}{K_d\,g\,P}\min\!\left(Z\sigma_{allow},\ T_{motor}\,i\,\eta\right)$$

To optimise the shares too - say, minimise arm mass at fixed reach and payload - load Solver, set shares and wall thicknesses as variables, constrain B15 ≤ 1, and minimise arm mass.

7 · Additional calculations worth adding

Dynamics - usually the real limit

Static torque rarely sizes a motor. Add a trapezoidal motion profile (target joint speed ω, acceleration time ta) and compute the inertia term, where J for each link about the joint is:

$$J=\tfrac{1}{3}mL^2+\sum m_{distal}d^2 \qquad T_{req}=T_{gravity}+J\alpha+T_{friction}$$

Then check RMS torque across the duty cycle against the motor's continuous rating - peak torque is a few-second figure, and steppers lose torque steeply with speed, so overlay your torque–speed curve.

Natural frequency

The arm will wobble long before it breaks. Keep fn above roughly 5× your fastest commanded motion frequency, or you will chase oscillation forever in tuning.

$$f_n\approx\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{3EI}{mL^3}}$$

Buckling

Any link in compression - common in parallelogram or linkage-driven designs - needs an Euler check. K = 1 pinned, 0.5 fixed–fixed.

$$P_{cr}=\frac{\pi^2EI}{(KL)^2}$$

Torsion

When the base rotates with the arm extended sideways, links twist. Open or thin-wall printed sections are very poor in torsion.

$$\tau=\frac{Tr}{J_p} \qquad \phi=\frac{TL}{GJ_p}$$

FDM-specific derates

  • Print links along their length so loads run in-plane, never across layers.
  • Four or more perimeters with sparse infill beats dense infill for bending stiffness per gram.
  • PLA creeps badly under sustained load and softens near 55 °C beside a warm stepper. If a pose is held for minutes, derate PLA to about 40% of yield or move to PETG/ASA.
  • Use heat-set inserts at every fastener, and check bolt pull-out and bearing stress at the joints - that is where printed arms actually fail first.

Joint compliance

Gearbox backlash, belt stretch and bearing play often exceed link deflection. Add a per-joint torsional stiffness (N·m/rad) and sum θ × reach into your error budget alongside the structural droop.

Accuracy budget

Combine encoder or step resolution × reach, backlash, deflection and thermal drift into one repeatability figure, then compare it against your task tolerance.

Payload modelling

Enter the payload's centre-of-gravity offset from the flange, not just its mass. A 500 g gripper holding a part 80 mm out adds real moment and real inertia.

Safe states

Check holding torque at power loss - or specify a brake or worm drive - and verify the arm cannot back-drive into a collapse. Add an E-stop and current limiting.

8 · Validation checklist

Hang known weights

In the worst-case pose, measure droop with a dial indicator and calibrate k against reality.

Confirm in FEA

Fusion 360 or FreeCAD. The spreadsheet models ideal beams and ignores stress concentrations at the joints - exactly where real failures start.

Check the thermal case

Measure link temperature next to the motors after a long duty cycle and compare with the Tg column.

Test the held pose

Leave the arm loaded and extended for an hour, then re-measure. Creep is invisible in any static calculation.

Disclaimer. This is an engineering aid built on idealised beam theory and indicative material properties. It ignores stress concentrations, joint and fastener detail, fatigue, creep and thermal effects. Verify any safety-critical or load-bearing design with FEA and physical testing before use.
